# 13 — Conceptual & Message-Driven Images (the idea is the hero)

The strongest contest VISION is often an IDEA, not a pretty subject. A conceptual image makes the viewer THINK and FEEL: social commentary, irony, role-reversal, juxtaposition of opposites, self-confrontation, the unexpected twist. These beat technically-pretty wildlife because most entrants do pretty — few do ideas that stop you and stay with you. This is the artist's natural voice; lean into it.

## Patterns that land
- **Juxtaposition of opposites in one frame** — rich/poor, free/caged, sacred/secular, human/machine, calm/danger.
- **Role reversal** — animals visiting a human zoo; predator at peace with prey; the powerful made helpless.
- **Self-confrontation / doppelgänger** — two versions of one person/face (poor self vs rich self).
- **Split-frame or seamless blend of two worlds/times** — child/adult, prayer/battlefield, surface/depth.
- **The ironic sign or caption inside the frame** — "HOMO SAPIENS"; "NO SHELTER FOR ANIMALS".
- **A punchy magazine headline that delivers the thesis** — often a question: "WHO IS HAPPIER?", "PROGRESS?".

## Execution rules (so the idea survives the render)
- The emotion must be **VISIBLE** — the inversion read on faces and body language (the bored-rich vs the joyful-poor), not merely implied.
- Multi-person / multi-element scenes are anatomy-risky: keep expressions candid, hands occupied or relaxed, inspect every face and hand at 100%, generate a batch and pick the clean frame.
- Two-versions-of-one-face needs **"identical facial features, the same person"** stated explicitly.
- Keep in-image SIGNS short, ALL-CAPS, English; the headline is the hero text — secondary signs are the typography risk (drop a numeric timer/long sign before the headline).
- Validated in generation: multi-person social-commentary scenes (the human-zoo, "who is happier") render beautifully and read instantly — the voice works.

## IP — DE-BRAND EVERYTHING (a contest DISQUALIFIER)
The contest requires work "without infringing on third-party intellectual property." AI defaults to rendering real logos — a KFC-style bucket, a Doritos-style bag, a Ralph Lauren polo, Apple/IBM marks, copyrighted TV-show characters. These are trademark/IP infringement and can disqualify the entry. RULE: specify **"generic / unbranded / no logo"** for every product, garment, device, and package; **rename trademarked entities** ("DEEP BLUE" → "THE MACHINE"); never depict copyrighted characters or branded show imagery. Make every reference transformative and generic. (Pop-culture "fandom battle" concepts — iPhone-vs-Android, show-vs-show — are the highest IP risk and the weakest gravitas; cut or fully abstract them.)

## English text only (this artist's hard rule)
ALL in-image text is ENGLISH — Hebrew and other scripts garble in every model (proven: a shelter sign rendered "מכל מוגן"). Foreign-language or religious objects (a prayer book, a Torah) must show NO legible lettering; keep them plain/closed.
