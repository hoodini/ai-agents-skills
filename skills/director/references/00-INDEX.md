# The Director's Bible — Index

> A complete, end-to-end knowledge base for conceiving, directing, and producing films — built to be **AI-native**. It covers the craft as award-winning directors practice it (story, camera, sound, editing), the *honest* psychology of why it works (not the pop-science version), the full professional pipeline, and how to execute every piece of it with current (mid-2026) AI image, video, and audio models. It is designed to become the brain of a Claude skill.

**Audience:** an advanced, AI-native maker. Every chapter teaches the *why*, then ends with a `→ AI APPLICATION` bridge translating the principle into model choice + prompt syntax.

**Total: ~108,000 words across 27 chapters + this index.** (Chapters 20–27 are the screenwriting wing — added because *the script is the story, and the story is where films are won or lost.*)

---

## How to read it

- **In a hurry / making something now?** Read **Chapter 19** (the workflow) and follow it. It pulls from every other chapter at the right moment. Everything else is reference you consult *as the workflow tells you to.*
- **Learning the craft?** Read 01 → 13 in order (story → psychology → camera → sound → editing → pipeline).
- **Want to nail the story/script (the thing that's actually been failing)?** Read **Chapter 27** (the gated, rung-by-rung script process) and the screenwriting wing **20 → 26**. This is where most films are won or lost — before a single shot is designed.
- **Just need the AI execution layer?** Read 14 → 18 (image models → video models → consistency → prompting/keyframes → audio).

The single most important idea in the whole bible: **films fail on decisions made too late, not on weak craft.** Lock the spine in words before you generate a single pixel. That is the entire point of Chapter 19.

This bible adds: rigorous film-school craft, *honest* neuroscience (it corrects several claims the creator space states as fact), the current AI-model selection layer, and the gated grilling procedure (Ch. 19). When the two overlap, the bible goes deeper or more accurate; it does not repeat the manipulation tables.

## ⚠️ Currency watchlist — re-verify before the skill ships or before quoting a fact

Story, camera, sound, and editing craft (Ch. 01–13) are stable for decades. **The AI layer (Ch. 14–18) drifts monthly.** The research surfaced 74 specific open questions; condensed:

1. **Model versions & names** — Veo, Kling, Runway, Luma, Seedance, MiniMax, Midjourney, Nano Banana, Flux all version fast. Re-confirm the current major version before relying on a cited feature.
2. **Sora status** — multiple sources indicate the Sora 2 / 2 Pro **API is scheduled to sunset ~Sep 24 2026** (consumer app earlier). A successor is likely but unconfirmed. **Do not build a Sora-dependent pipeline without re-verifying availability.**
3. **First/last-frame & keyframe support** — confirmed for Veo 3.1, Kling 3, Luma Ray3 (up to 16 keyframes), Pika Pikaframes, Seedance i2v; **endpoint-dependent for Hailuo**; verify on the *specific* host (fal/Replicate/native) before relying on last-frame.
4. **Midjourney character reference** — `--cref` is V6/Niji6 legacy; omni-reference (`--oref`) was V7; verify whether V8.x has backported a character-reference feature before depending on it.
5. **Pricing** — every per-second/per-image figure is order-of-magnitude; re-check live pricing pages before quoting a budget.
6. **AI-music legality** — the Suno/Udio vs Sony case has a **pivotal hearing ~July 2026**; commercial-rights guidance for AI music may shift. Both vendors are reportedly building licensed-data models.
7. **Platform delivery specs** — loudness (~-14 LUFS), file/bitrate caps, safe areas drift; confirm against each platform's creator docs at delivery time.
8. **Soft-science thresholds** — the "3-second retention cutoffs" and "5–8 s re-hook cadence" in Ch. 04 are directional heuristics, not measured constants; A/B-test per format.
